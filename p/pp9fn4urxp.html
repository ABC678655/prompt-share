Create a premium vertical Zenless Zone Zero character portrait poster based on extremely low-key cinematic anime lighting and a narrow beam of naturally refracted sunlight.



The character exists primarily inside deep darkness.



Only a narrow shaft of refracted natural light reveals selected parts of the face, hair, neck, and upper body.



The central lighting concept should combine:



a bright predominantly WHITE natural-light core,



very restrained spectral coloration while the light crosses the face,



and progressively stronger wavelength separation as the light travels farther away from the facial focal region.



The result should look elegant, simple, optically coherent, and cinematic.



IMPORTANT:



Do NOT create a rigid seven-color rainbow stripe.



Do NOT create chaotic broken rainbow fragments.



Do NOT create multiple scattered caustics.



Do NOT cover the character's face with strongly saturated rainbow colors.



The face should primarily be illuminated by WHITE or warm-white sunlight.



Spectral dispersion becomes increasingly visible only farther away from the face.



ASPECT RATIO:

9:16 vertical.

2. CORE VISUAL CONCEPT

==================================================



Create a dramatically underexposed anime portrait against an almost completely black background.



Most of the character remains hidden inside darkness.



Only a narrow refracted-light path reveals selected surfaces.



The portrait should rely on:



deep black negative space,

extreme low-key exposure,

large coherent shadow masses,

one narrow vertically oriented light path,

a bright natural white-light center,

subtle optical dispersion,

minimal ambient illumination,

strong cinematic chiaroscuro.



Approximately 70–85% of the visible character should remain in deep shadow or near-black values.



The final image should NOT resemble:



a normally illuminated character with a colorful stripe placed over the image.



Instead:



the light itself should be responsible for revealing the character.





==================================================

3. EXPOSURE

==================================================



Use intentionally low exposure.



Expose for the bright refracted light rather than for the entire character.



Areas outside direct illumination should appear approximately 2.5–4 stops darker than the directly illuminated regions.



Do NOT automatically recover shadow detail.



Do NOT brighten the whole face.



Do NOT illuminate the entire hairstyle.



Do NOT brighten clothing simply to display costume details.



Deep darkness is intentional.



Some hair, shoulders, and costume regions may disappear substantially into the black background.





==================================================

4. NO FILL LIGHT

==================================================



Use almost no conventional fill light.



Avoid:



front fill light,

beauty lighting,

softbox lighting,

broad studio illumination,

ambient face lighting,

strong bounce light,

The shadow side of the face should remain genuinely dark.



One shoulder may nearly disappear.



Large areas of hair may merge into the black background.



Only extremely subtle reflected illumination may exist near directly illuminated surfaces.





==================================================

5. BACKGROUND

==================================================



Use an almost pure black background.



No visible environment.



No room.

No city.

No architecture.

No decorative fantasy scenery.



The background should feel like deep black empty space.



Use rich OLED-like black values.



An almost imperceptible undertone from the character's palette is acceptable, but the immediate impression must remain BLACK.



Large black negative space is essential.

Maintain elegant believable anatomy.



The pose should feel like the character is quietly looking toward a distant light source above.

==================================================

10. PRIMARY LIGHT — NATURAL REFRACTED SUNLIGHT

==================================================



Create ONE narrow refracted shaft of natural sunlight.



Imagine warm daylight passing through a clear beveled glass prism or faceted transparent crystal located outside the frame.



The resulting projected light travels through the composition in a mostly vertical or very slightly diagonal direction.



The overall path should remain clean and visually simple.



Do NOT create a chaotic light pattern.



Do NOT create multiple separate spectral fragments.



The light may pass through:



upper empty space,

hair,

forehead,

one eye or the region beside one eye,

cheek,

jaw,

neck,

collar,

upper torso.



The exact placement should adapt naturally to the character.





==================================================

11. OPTICAL STRUCTURE — MOST IMPORTANT

==================================================



The refracted light should have THREE visual zones.



ZONE A — FAR ABOVE THE FACE:



spectral separation is visible,

but still elegant and controlled.



A mostly unified light beam begins to show:



a warm red-orange fringe on one side,

a pale yellow-white central region,

a restrained cyan-blue-violet fringe on the opposite side.



The colors remain soft and partially overlapping.



ZONE B — ACROSS THE FACE:



the different wavelengths overlap much more strongly.



The beam becomes primarily:



bright white,

warm white,

ivory,

pale golden-white.



Only very thin and subtle spectral edges remain.



The facial illumination should therefore appear mostly WHITE.



ZONE C — BELOW THE FACE:



as the light continues traveling away from the facial overlap region,

the wavelengths gradually separate again.



Color dispersion becomes increasingly visible across:



neck,

collar,

upper chest,

lower image area.



The farther the light travels from the facial overlap region,

the more visible the spectral separation may become.





==================================================

12. FACE = WHITE-LIGHT DOMINANT

==================================================



This is critical.



While crossing the character's face:



approximately 80–90% of the visual impression should be bright natural white or warm-white illumination.



Only approximately 10–20% should read as colored spectral edging.



The face must NOT look rainbow-colored.



The brightest area should appear:



white,

ivory,

pale gold,

warm sunlight.



Spectral coloration should appear mainly as:



thin red-orange edging,

very subtle cyan-blue edging,

occasional faint violet fringe.



Keep these colored fringes secondary.



The viewer should first perceive:



beautiful white sunlight across the face.



Only after closer inspection should they notice subtle prism dispersion.





==================================================

13. DISPERSION INCREASES WITH DISTANCE

==================================================



The amount of visible wavelength separation should depend on distance from the facial overlap region.



Near the face:



strong overlap,

mostly white.



Moderately away from the face:



slightly wider colored fringes.



Farther away:



clearer spectral separation.



This progression should be smooth.



Do not suddenly switch from white to rainbow.



Do not use constant spectral separation from top to bottom.



The optical effect should feel continuous and physically motivated.





==================================================

14. ABOVE-HEAD DISPERSION

==================================================



In the black negative space above the head, the refracted light may gradually reveal clearer color separation.



Keep the pattern clean.



Use approximately:



one warm side,

one bright pale center,

one cool side.



For example:



soft red-orange fringe,



bright warm-white / pale-yellow core,



subtle cyan-blue-violet fringe.



Do NOT show seven equally separated bands.



Do NOT create textbook ROYGBIV stripes.



The upper spectrum should feel elegant and photographic.





==================================================

15. LOWER DISPERSION

==================================================



After the light passes the face and travels downward across:

neck,

collar,

The lower section can contain more visible:

However:

Do not allow the lower spectrum to become a fully saturated rainbow banner.





==================================================

16. SIMPLE SPECTRAL ORGANIZATION

==================================================



Keep the spectral structure visually organized.



Use one dominant warm side and one dominant cool side.

==================================================

As the projected light propagates farther away from this plane:



different wavelengths become progressively more spatially separated.



Therefore:



FACE = predominantly white light.



FARTHER ABOVE / BELOW FACE = increasingly visible dispersion.



This physical relationship is the defining lighting rule of the image.
