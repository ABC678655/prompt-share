CHARACTER NAME:

“[角色名字]”



OPTIONAL ENGLISH / ROMANIZED NAME:

“[英文名或罗马音；如果没有就删除这一行]”



Use the provided character reference as the primary and authoritative visual identity reference.



The character name defined above is the ONLY canonical character name for this image.



Use that exact name whenever visible name typography is required.



Do not invent alternate spellings.

Do not introduce unrelated names.

Do not repeat or reinterpret the character name elsewhere unless needed for visible typography.



Faithfully preserve the reference character’s recognizable facial features, face shape, eye shape and eye color, hairstyle, hair color, signature accessories, costume motifs, characteristic color palette, and overall visual identity.



Do not redesign the reference character into a generic anime character.



Create a premium painterly anime portrait with strong character-specific art direction.



## CHARACTER / POSE



Show the character in an upper-body composition, approximately chest-up or waist-up.



The character faces the viewer in a relaxed near-frontal or three-quarter angle.



Raise both hands naturally in front of the face and form a loose rectangular / camera-frame gesture using the fingers.



The hands should frame the eyes and central facial area without covering the character’s recognizable facial features.



Use:



- both hands clearly visible

- correct number of fingers

- believable finger joints

- natural wrist angles

- slight asymmetry between the two hands

- relaxed and character-appropriate hand placement



The gesture should remain stylish and visually strong, but its exact attitude should adapt to the reference character.



For example:



a lively / playful character →

make the gesture slightly more energetic, mischievous and spontaneous.



a cool / reserved character →

use restrained hand movement, composed posture and stronger visual stillness.



a gentle / elegant character →

use softer fingers, relaxed shoulders and graceful hand positioning.



a confident / assertive character →

use a cleaner, stronger framing gesture with direct eye contact.



Do not force every character into the exact same pose attitude.



The basic hand-frame concept remains consistent, while the body language should feel naturally appropriate for the reference character.



## CHARACTER-ADAPTIVE EXPRESSION



Do NOT automatically use a calm, melancholic or moody expression.



First analyze the visible personality and emotional impression communicated by the provided character reference.



Infer the portrait’s emotional direction primarily from visible character-design cues such as:



- facial expression

- eye shape and gaze

- eyebrow shape

- mouth expression

- hairstyle rhythm

- costume design

- color palette

- accessories

- overall visual energy

- pose language



Then adapt the generated expression to feel consistent with that character.



The final expression should feel like a natural extension of the reference character rather than a generic editorial expression.



If the reference character appears cheerful, lively or energetic:



use bright expressive eyes,

slightly raised eyebrows,

subtle playful smile,

light teasing energy,

more open facial expression.



If the reference character appears mischievous, cunning or playful:



use slightly narrowed expressive eyes,

subtle asymmetric smile,

playful gaze,

small hint of teasing confidence.



If the reference character appears cool, quiet or emotionally restrained:



use calm direct gaze,

relaxed mouth,

slightly lowered eyelids,

controlled facial tension,

subtle emotional distance.



If the reference character appears gentle, warm or elegant:



use soft eyes,

relaxed eyebrows,

very slight smile,

gentle gaze,

calm facial muscles.



If the reference character appears confident, strong or dominant:



use steady direct eye contact,

slightly sharper eyes,

subtle confident mouth shape,

controlled posture,

strong visual presence.



If the reference character appears dreamy, mysterious or ethereal:



use slightly distant gaze,

soft eyelids,

delicate mouth expression,

subtle introspection,

quiet dreamlike emotional presence.



If the reference character already has a very recognizable signature expression, preserve and reinterpret that expression directly.



## EXPRESSION CONSISTENCY



The generated facial expression must remain compatible with the character’s visual identity.



Do not impose:



sadness on a cheerful character,

a broad smile on a reserved character,

aggressive confidence on a gentle character,

blank melancholy on a playful character,

overly cute behavior on a mature or elegant character.



Preserve the emotional identity of the reference character.



The viewer should feel:



“this is how this specific character would naturally behave in this portrait concept.”



rather than:



“the same preset expression was applied to another character.”



## EYES



The eyes are the primary emotional focal point.



Preserve the reference character’s:



eye shape,

eye color,

pupil design,

eyelash style,

and recognizable gaze quality.



Adapt only the eyelid position, eyebrow tension and gaze direction necessary to express the character-appropriate mood.



Possible gaze directions:



directly toward the viewer,

slightly through the finger frame,

slightly sideways,

slightly downward,

or subtly upward,



depending on what best matches the reference character.



Do not unnecessarily redesign the eyes.



## MOUTH / FACIAL TENSION



Adapt the mouth expression subtly.



Possible treatments include:



tiny relaxed smile,

neutral closed mouth,

slightly asymmetric smirk,

soft parted lips,

subtle playful smile.



Keep expressions controlled and character-specific.



Avoid exaggerated open-mouth expressions unless the reference character is clearly energetic and expressive.



Avoid generic “sad anime face.”



## CHARACTER-ADAPTIVE COLOR SYSTEM



Do NOT automatically use a blue palette.



Analyze the reference character’s palette first.



Identify:



1. PRIMARY COLOR

The most recognizable dominant character color, usually derived from hair, outfit, or signature design.



2. SECONDARY COLOR

A supporting color from the costume, accessories, eyes, or secondary hair elements.



3. ACCENT COLOR

A smaller but visually distinctive color used in eyes, ribbons, mechanical parts, jewelry, trim, or signature ornaments.



Build the entire portrait around these colors.



Examples:



red-haired character →

deep crimson + burgundy + black / gray + restrained warm highlights



pink-themed character →

dusty rose + muted magenta + cream / charcoal



cyan-haired character →

ice cyan + pale blue-gray + deep navy + restrained yellow / gold accents



purple-themed character →

muted violet + lavender-gray + deep plum



green-themed character →

jade green + muted teal + dark charcoal + pale mint



Do NOT force the reference character into an unrelated color scheme.



## BACKGROUND COLOR



Create a simple minimalist background derived directly from the reference character’s palette.



Use the PRIMARY COLOR as the main background family.



Adjust its value and saturation so the character remains clearly separated from the background.



Recommended logic:



if the character has a bright/light primary color →

use a darker, muted version of that hue in the background.



if the character has a dark primary color →

use a lighter, softer or desaturated version of that hue.



if the character has multiple equally important colors →

create a restrained two-tone gradient or large soft color blocks using the two dominant hues.



The background should remain simple.



Possible treatments:



- smooth monochromatic gradient

- large soft color field

- subtle two-tone gradient

- faint broad painterly shapes

- very restrained atmospheric wash



Do not create a detailed environment.



No scenery.



No complex room.



No random objects.



The background exists only to reinforce the character’s color identity.



## CAP — CHARACTER-SPECIFIC COLOR MATCHING



The character wears a stylish baseball cap.



Do NOT always make the cap black.



Choose the cap color based on the reference character.



The cap should use either:



- the darkest version of the character’s PRIMARY COLOR,

or

- the character’s characteristic dark neutral color,

or

- the SECONDARY COLOR when it creates stronger visual balance.



Examples:



red / pink character →

deep burgundy, dark wine red, charcoal-red, or muted black-red



cyan / blue character →

deep navy, blue-gray, dark cyan



purple character →

deep plum, muted violet-black



green character →

dark teal, forest green, charcoal-green



white / silver character →

charcoal gray, cool gray, deep secondary accent color



The cap should clearly belong to the character’s own visual palette.



Add restrained contrasting stitching or small accent details using the ACCENT COLOR.



The front of the cap may display the text:



“ZENLESS ZONE ZERO”



in small clean lettering.



Keep the cap text secondary.



Do not let it compete with the character-name typography.



The cap should sit naturally on the head.



Preserve enough of the original hairstyle underneath and around the cap for immediate character recognition.



Do not completely hide signature hair accessories unless physically necessary.



## HAIR



Faithfully preserve the reference hairstyle.



Render the hair through large, coherent painterly anime locks.



Use:



clean major hair masses,

controlled loose strands,

broad expressive brushwork,

subtle edge softness,

character-specific highlights.



Do not turn the hair into thousands of individual realistic strands.



Do not replace the original hairstyle with a generic cap hairstyle.



## CHARACTER-ADAPTIVE LIGHTING



Adapt the lighting intensity and softness to the character’s emotional style.



The lighting color should still derive from the character-specific palette.



A lively character may receive:



cleaner highlights,

slightly brighter facial illumination,

more energetic accent light.



A calm or elegant character may receive:



soft directional illumination,

controlled highlights,

gentle transitions.



A cool or serious character may receive:



slightly stronger directional contrast,

restrained rim light,

deeper controlled shadows.



A dreamy character may receive:



soft diffused illumination,

slight atmospheric glow,

delicate edge light.



Always keep the face readable and attractive.



Do not let mood lighting obscure the character’s identity.



## PAINTERLY ANIME STYLE



Use a premium painterly anime / game illustration style.



Combine:



refined anime facial construction,

clean expressive eyes,

broad loose brush strokes,

controlled painterly shading,

slightly sketchy secondary edges,

softly simplified shadow masses,

selectively unfinished peripheral details.



Important focal areas should remain highly refined:



eyes,

face,

hands,

cap,

signature hair details,

important character accessories.



Allow secondary areas to become gradually looser:



outer hair,

shoulders,

clothing edges,

background.



The image should feel artistic and hand-painted without becoming dirty or chaotic.



Avoid heavy oil-paint impasto.



Avoid rough canvas texture.



Avoid uncontrolled scribbles.



## TYPOGRAPHY — USE THE NAME DEFINED AT THE TOP



Display the character’s name prominently as an integrated artistic typography element.



Use ONLY the exact character name defined at the top of this prompt.



Do not invent another name.



Do not create alternate spellings.



If an English / Romanized name was provided at the top, use that as the primary large wordmark.



Use the original character name as a smaller secondary subtitle.



If only one name was provided, use that single name consistently.



## TYPOGRAPHY STYLE



The name should look like deliberate graphic design rather than plain text.



Use a sophisticated artistic wordmark treatment inspired by:



modern Japanese anime key visuals,

fashion editorial typography,

game character branding,

music album cover design,

premium character posters.



Possible typography characteristics:



- custom stretched lettering

- elegant serif + modern sans-serif combination

- wide letter spacing

- overlapping letters

- partially cropped oversized letters

- thin outline typography

- subtle handwritten accent

- geometric letter construction

- controlled vertical / horizontal alignment



Do not make the lettering overly decorative or difficult to read.



The character name should remain recognizable and readable.



## TYPOGRAPHY COLOR



Automatically adapt the typography color to the reference character palette.



Possible logic:



dark background →

use off-white / pale character accent color for the main name.



light background →

use deep character primary / secondary color.



For selected letters or small details, use the character’s ACCENT COLOR.



Avoid unrelated rainbow typography.



The lettering must visually belong to the same color system as the character, cap, and background.



## TYPOGRAPHY PLACEMENT



Place the character name in an area that complements the portrait without covering the face.



Recommended placements:



- large partially cropped lettering behind the character

- lower-left corner

- vertical text along one edge

- upper-left editorial placement

- large name extending across the lower background

- split typography around the silhouette



The text may partially pass behind the character to create depth.



Keep the eyes and facial features unobstructed.



## COLOR COHERENCE



The following elements must feel deliberately color-matched:



character

+

background

+

cap

+

typography

+

lighting accents.



They should all derive from the reference character’s original visual palette.



Do not introduce a dominant unrelated hue.



The result should feel as if the entire portrait was art-directed specifically for this character.



## FINAL MOOD



Do NOT lock the image to a fixed mood.



Derive the emotional atmosphere from the provided reference character.



The final artwork may feel:



playful,

confident,

cool,

gentle,

elegant,

energetic,

dreamy,

mysterious,

quiet,

mischievous,

warm,

or restrained,



depending on the reference character.



Expression,

pose,

background,

cap,

lighting,

brushwork,

and typography should all support the same character-specific mood.

Avoid:



generic anime face,

incorrect character identity,

different hairstyle,

wrong hair color,

wrong eye color,

unrelated background color,

fixed blue palette regardless of character,

always-black cap regardless of character palette,

generic melancholy expression,

generic sleepy expression,

personality mismatch,

random neon colors,

rainbow background,

busy scenery,

complex environment,

hands covering the eyes,

extra fingers,

missing fingers,

fused fingers,

broken wrists,

duplicate hands,

deformed anatomy,

oversized hands,

tiny hands,

cap hiding the entire hairstyle,

incorrect character-name spelling,

random letters,

fake typography,

unreadable major name,

text covering the face,

too much text,

paragraphs,

dirty brushwork.



## CLEAN RENDERING / IMAGE QUALITY



Render with an exceptionally clean, polished, high-end anime/game illustration finish.



Use smooth and controlled color transitions, clean cel-shading, precise edges, clear facial features, and large coherent areas of color. Keep the skin, hair, clothing, typography, and background visually clean and intentionally rendered.



The image should look crisp and professionally finished rather than artificially textured.



Avoid:



- visible digital noise

- film grain

- colored grain

- luminance grain

- dirty speckles

- gritty texture

- sand-like texture

- paper texture

- canvas texture

- random micro-texture

- excessive skin texture

- noisy hair strands

- muddy shadows

- dirty gradients

- blotchy shading

- chromatic noise

- JPEG-like artifacts

- oversharpening halos

- excessive micro-contrast



Skin should be smooth and clean with controlled anime-style shading rather than photographic pores or grain.



Hair should be rendered as clean, coherent locks and shapes rather than thousands of noisy individual strands.



For the painterly treatment, express visible brushwork through broad shape transitions, simplified edges, deliberate large strokes, and selective softness rather than grain or dirty surface texture.



Shadows should remain smooth, intentional, and clearly shaped.



Background gradients should remain clean and controlled.



Character-name typography should have crisp edges and strong readability.



Prioritize:



clean shapes,

smooth surfaces,

controlled detail,

coherent hair masses,

clean cel shading,

broad painterly strokes,

smooth gradients,

crisp expressive eyes,

accurate hands,

precise typography,

polished edges,

high visual clarity.



The final image should resemble a professionally art-directed premium anime/game character portrait with a custom character-matched color palette, matching cap design, integrated artistic name typography, character-consistent emotion, and a pristine digital finish.
